# CH-01 exclusion ladder — govinfo `<EDNOTE>` → the usable pool

Generated by `docs/evidence/ch01-pool/ch01_pool.py` from `data/ednotes/counts.json`.
Its stdout is committed beside it as `ch01-pool-run.txt`. Every rung asserts that
**kept + removed == received**; the script exits non-zero if any rung fails to sum,
so a ladder that reaches the bottom is a ladder that adds up (hard rule 14).

Source: `https://www.govinfo.gov/bulkdata/ECFR`. `www.ecfr.gov` and
`www.federalregister.gov` return HTTP 403 from this machine and were not used.

### The fourth: 36 against 38, and why it is a definition rather than a defect

`prompts/CH-01.md` step 5 defines section-level as **"not appendix/part"** — a
question about the note's *container*. `CONTEXT.md` §8's own table answers a
different question: its rows *Section-level 38/44* and *Localise below section
level 6/44* sum to 44, so its 38 counts notes that **localise to a named section**,
wherever the note itself physically sits.

Both readings are computed. On the nine reference titles:

| Reading | Nine titles | Full corpus |
|---|---:|---:|
| **A** — sits inside `<DIV8 TYPE="SECTION">` *(the prompt's, and the gate's)* | 36 | 86 |
| **B** — A, or else names a section in its own prose *(the reference's)* | 38 | 88 |

Reading B returns **38** on the nine titles — the reference
figure, exactly. The gap is two notes, both real, both identified:

- **title 40, part 63**, container `APPENDIX`, cites `89 FR 43274` — names its section in prose but does not sit in one.
- **title 49, part 383**, container `PART`, cites `90 FR 46525` — names its section in prose but does not sit in one.

**The pool gate is computed on reading A, the smaller of the two.** Reading B
would give 88 section-level rather than 86, and 87 usable rather than 85. Both clear the threshold, so nothing
turns on the choice — which is exactly why it is recorded now, before anything
depends on it, rather than settled later in whichever direction helps.

### Why the projection overshot, with the arithmetic

`CONTEXT.md` §8's range is a per-title extrapolation from the nine reference
titles: 44 notes ÷ 9 titles × 50 titles = **244**. That step
assumes the nine are typical. They are the **largest**:

| | Nine reference titles | Full corpus |
|---|---:|---:|
| Titles | 9 (18%) | 49 |
| Raw XML bytes | 408,351,996 (**50%**) | 824,289,052 |
| Defect notes | 44 (**41%**) | 107 |

They include title 40, the largest at 161 MB, and title 26, the second largest.
Nine titles that are 18% of the corpus by count but **50% by bytes** cannot
carry a per-title extrapolation, and this one missed by **2.28×**.

### And that same factor was attributed to the wrong cause

`CONTEXT.md` §8 states the eCFR search API's figure of 92
*"undercounts by ~2.3×"*. Measured on govinfo and republished beside it rather
than in place of it, as `prompts/CH-01.md` step 5 requires:

| | |
|---|---:|
| eCFR search API | 92 |
| govinfo, this chunk | **107** |
| Actual ratio | **1.16×** |
| Over-projection factor of the nine-title extrapolation | **2.28×** |

The eCFR API undercounts by **1.16×**, not 2.3×. The
**2.28×** belongs to the extrapolation, not to the API. The two figures
were reconciled by assuming the API was wrong, when what was wrong was the
arithmetic that produced the expectation — 92 was far closer to
the truth than the range built to discredit it.

This is hard rule 15 — *verify before you relay* — firing on this project's own
spec. Neither figure was tuned and both are printed. `CONTEXT.md` is protected
read-only for a build session, so the correction is recorded here and in
`PROGRESS.md` for the architect rather than applied.

## The one defect note with no FR citation — an upstream typo, not a parser miss

Rung 4 removes **1** note of 107. `CONTEXT.md` §8 records 44/44 on the
nine reference titles and that reproduces exactly; across all 49 titles the rate
is 106/107. The single exception was read back from the source:

> **Title 47, part 54, § 54.503** — *“At 83 FR , May 1,2018, § 54.503 was amended by revising paragraph (a)(6) however the agency provided two different paragraph (a)(6)'s, the amendment could not be incorporated due to inaccurate amendatory instruction.”*

The volume number is present and **the page number is simply missing from the
published note** (`At 83 FR , May 1,2018`). No extractor can resolve that to a
document. It is excluded, counted, and shown rather than quietly dropped; FR
resolution is deterministic for the other 106.

## Provenance

`data/ednotes/manifest.json` carries the SHA-256 of every frozen artefact **and of
every raw title XML the numbers came from**, so this ladder is pinned to specific
upstream bytes rather than to "govinfo, at some point". `data/ednotes/
source_index.json` carries govinfo's announced size and last-modified stamp per
title; it is deliberately outside the hashed set, because that stamp moves when a
title is re-published even if nothing that matters changed.

Reproduce: `python refetch.py` (network) or `python refetch.py --verify-only` (none).
