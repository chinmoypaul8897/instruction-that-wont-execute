REMOVED CAPABILITY 3 - the ORDERED-STATE LEDGER (ruling R-01, counted removal)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Condition: a designation is touched by TWO OR MORE instructions in the item,
  so instruction k+1 reads the state instructions 1..k left behind.

  FOUR readings of that one sentence, strictest first. Every one is computed
  because no single one reproduces the published figure:

    reading                                        count      of     rate
    A  same designation touched twice (literal)       83   2,527     3.3%
    B  later path is a prefix/descendant             280   2,527    11.1%
    C  >1 instruction naming any designation         495   2,527    19.6%
    D  >1 instruction at all (the ceiling)           760   2,527    30.1%

    PUBLISHED FIGURE                              833   1,984    42.0%   (CONTEXT.md §6)

  *** THE 42.0% DOES NOT REPRODUCE. ***

  Not under the literal reading, not under any of the three looser ones, and
  not over any denominator this corpus yields: the shipped attribution has
  2,527 items under v11 and 2,154 under the spec-literal rule. NEITHER IS
  1,984. The loosest reading conceivable - 'the item has more than one
  instruction' - reaches only 30.1%, so 42.0% is above this measurement's
  CEILING, not merely outside its range.

  WHAT IS AND IS NOT CONCLUDED. It is NOT concluded that 42.0% is wrong: it may
  have been computed over a corpus or a definition this session cannot see, and
  hard rule 15 forbids relaying a contradiction as a refutation. What IS
  concluded is that THE FIGURE IS NOT REPRODUCIBLE FROM THE SHIPPED ARTIFACTS,
  so it cannot carry a claim in the submission. It is raised as QUESTIONS.md
  Q23 and is not quoted as settled anywhere.

  THE REMOVAL DECISION IS UNAFFECTED, and that is worth stating plainly: ruling
  R-01 cut the ledger to measure two capabilities properly rather than three
  in a hurry. That reasoning never depended on the class size, which is why
  discovering the number is unreliable costs the decision nothing. A removal
  justified by a number that turns out not to reproduce would have been a
  worse position to be in than this one.

REMOVED EXPERIMENT 2 - the INTRA-RULE COLLISION DETECTOR
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Condition: the item contains a REDESIGNATE instruction whose designation is
  also touched by another instruction in the same item.

    items containing ANY redesignation   82 of 2,527   = 3.2%
    COLLISION fires on                   43 of 2,527   = 1.70%

  against the two prior figures, NEITHER of which this reproduces exactly:
    pilot            26 of 1,984 = 1.31%   difference +0.39 pp
    naive recount    61 of 1,984 = 3.07%   difference -1.37 pp

  PUBLISHED AS MEASURED. CONTEXT.md §10 pre-committed to publishing whatever
  the shipped script yields, with the discrepancy stated, and that is what
  this is. The 1.3%-3.1% range is NOT narrowed to a single settled figure by
  this run; a third number is added to it, and the honest summary is that
  THE CLASS SIZE DOES NOT REPRODUCE ACROSS IMPLEMENTATIONS.

  WHY THAT DOES NOT CHANGE THE DECISION. Every one of the three figures is
  small, and the removal never rested on the exact value. It rested on:
    - 0/68 labelled items contain a redesignation instruction;
    - NARA never publishes a note naming an intra-rule conflict - a live probe
      for "conflicting amendments" returned 0;
    - and 15 of the pilot's 26 collisions are CORRECT DRAFTING, not defects.
  A detector for a class NARA does not write notes about cannot be scored
  against NARA's notes, whatever its class size.
